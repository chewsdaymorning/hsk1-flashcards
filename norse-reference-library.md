### What the evidence ACTUALLY shows (design dos & don'ts)
- **Viking art styles (the authentic visual vocabulary):** Oseberg → Borre → Jellinge → Mammen → Ringerike → Urnes. Overviews: Smarthistory, World History Encyclopedia (Viking Art), Khan Academy. **Best for working designers:** Jonas Lau Markussen, "The Anatomy of Viking Art" (jonaslaumarkussen.com) — anatomical breakdowns per style (his illustrations are his IP — learn the method, make original work).
- **Thor's hammers:** ~1,000 found, mostly women's graves; real form = short asymmetric shaft, T-head (NOT the symmetric pop version). Købelev find inscribed "this is a hammer." Wikimedia Mjöllnir record; thehistoryblog.com/archives/31199.
- **Brooches/dress:** paired oval (tortoise) brooches = the most abundant female find (Petersen typology); wool/linen, bright natural dyes, silk = elite import. Hurstwic clothing; Birka textiles.
- **DON'T:** horned helmets (Bronze-Age ritual, popularized by an 1876 Wagner opera — only one Viking helmet survives: **Gjermundbu**, a spectacle/nasal iron cap). Vegvísir/Ægishjálmur as "Viking" (post-medieval — see Part B). Generic "Celtic" knotwork mislabeled Viking.

### Realistic reconstruction (evidence-based, not fantasy)
- **Hurstwic** (hurstwic.org) — evidence-grounded daily life, caveats flagged.
- **Viking Ship Museum Roskilde** — experimental archaeology, real ship finds.
- **Regia Anglorum** (regia.org) — academically respected reenactment standards.

## PART B — ACCURATE EXPLANATIONS & CITABLE SOURCES (for copy & the history cards)

### Primary texts (free) — cite THESE, not secondary sites
- **Poetic Edda** (Bellows 1936) — sacred-texts.com/neu/poe/ *(public domain; dated but citable)*
- **Prose Edda** (Brodeur 1916) — sacred-texts.com/neu/pre/
- **Modern standard translations to cite:** Jackson Crawford (2015) & Carolyne Larrington (OUP 2014).
- **Sagas:** Icelandic Saga Database (sagadb.org); Heimskringla.no.
- **Skaldic verse:** skaldic.org (peer-reviewed).

### THE RUNE POEMS (the historical basis for rune meanings — your card citations)
- **Wikisource — Rune Poems** (Dickins 1915, all three) — en.wikisource.org/wiki/Rune_poems
- **Ragweedforge** — ragweedforge.com/poems.html (facing translations)
- **Icelandic Rune Poem (VSNR, peer-reviewed PDF)** — vsnrweb-publications.org.uk/The%20Icelandic%20Rune-Poem.pdf
- **Citation pattern for product cards:** *"Fehu — 'cattle/wealth,' Old English Rune Poem, c. 9th c."* This beats any New Age source and instantly separates you from Blum-based competitors.

### Source-aware explainers (good leads; cite the scholars they cite)
- **Norse Mythology for Smart People** (norse-mythology.org, Daniel McCoy)
- **World History Encyclopedia** (worldhistory.org)

- **Prof. Tom Birkett** (UCC; runes for general audiences).
- **American Association for Runic Studies** (runicstudies.org) — curated scholarly bibliography.

### The 6 accuracy distinctions to build copy around (each with a source)
1. **Elder vs Younger Futhark** — Vikings used the **Younger** (16 runes, ~800 CE); Elder (24) predates them. Source: Viking Ship Museum Roskilde; Runor DB. → *Honest line for the history card.*
2. **Vegvísir is NOT Viking** — first in the 1860 Huld Manuscript. Source: Projekt Forlǫg.
3. **Helm of Awe** — the *word* is in the Eddas; the *graphic* is a 17th-c. Galdrabók stave. Source: Brute Norse.
4. **Rune meanings = the rune poems**, not Ralph Blum's 1982 invention (his "blank rune" has no historical basis). Source: Wikisource rune poems.
5. **No horned helmets** — Bronze-Age ritual; Gjermundbu is the only Viking helmet. Source: ScienceNorway; Smithsonian.
6. **Eddic myth ≠ actual cult practice** — Eddas written 200+ yrs post-conversion by Christians. Source: Swedish History Museum; Hurstwic.

## PART C — HOW THIS PLUGS INTO THE DRAFT LISTING (placeholders → real citations)

- Rune-meaning cards → **Old English Rune Poem** (Wikisource/Ragweedforge), Icelandic poem (VSNR).
- History card "Elder vs Younger" honesty note → **Viking Ship Museum Roskilde** + **Runor**.
- "Vegvísir/Helm of Awe are post-Viking" explainer → **Projekt Forlǫg** + **Brute Norse**.
- Any artifact-replica jewelry → cite the actual find via **Unimus / Historiska / Wikimedia Mjöllnir record / PAS** (and use CC0 Met/Smithsonian imagery for mockups).
- Design motifs → name the real **art style** (Borre, Mammen, Urnes) per Smarthistory / Jonas Lau Markussen.

**Rule of thumb:** cite the *primary text or museum*, link it, and date it. That single habit is the brand's whole accuracy moat — and almost no competitor does it.
